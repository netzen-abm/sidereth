# The Purple Frog — Product Brand Identity

**Status:** CANONICAL PRODUCT-BRAND DECISION  
**Decision date:** 2026-09-06  
**Brand:** **The Purple Frog**

## 1. Product name

The public product/consumer-facing brand is:

> **The Purple Frog**

**SIDERETH** remains the underlying engineering ecosystem and technical architecture identity unless a separate explicit platform-brand decision changes that relationship.

```text
The Purple Frog
    ↓
Public product / brand
    ↓
SIDERETH
    ↓
Shared legal & regulatory infrastructure
    ↓
Capabilities · contracts · tools · resources · workflows · intelligence · adapters
```

The brand and the technical infrastructure are deliberately decoupled.

## 2. Visual identity — canonical concept

The Purple Frog logo is a combined emblem representing:

- **Gaur / Indian Bison (*Bos gaurus*)**
- **Purple Frog / Indian Purple Frog (*Nasikabatrachus sahyadrensis*)**

The combined visual identity is intentional. It must not be reduced in documentation to a Gaur-only emblem.

The two species are symbolic components of the brand identity; their scientific or conservation attributes must remain independently sourced when used as factual claims.

## 3. Conservation meaning

The brand uses biodiversity awareness as part of its public identity. The visual emblem is not itself a scientific conservation-status claim.

Public material must distinguish:

```text
brand symbolism
    ≠
scientific taxonomy
    ≠
conservation status
    ≠
conservation outcome
```

Any claim about a species' conservation status, distribution, population, threat level or legal protection must identify the relevant source, scope and date/version where applicable.

## 4. Species identity

### Gaur

**Common names:** Gaur / Indian Bison  
**Scientific name:** *Bos gaurus*

### Purple Frog

**Common names:** Purple Frog / Indian Purple Frog / Mahabali Frog / Pig-nose Frog  
**Scientific name:** *Nasikabatrachus sahyadrensis*

Common names can vary by source and geography. Scientific names and conservation data must be treated as structured, sourced data rather than hard-coded marketing claims.

## 5. Logo asset hierarchy

Preferred asset hierarchy:

1. **Canonical master vector** — the combined Gaur + Purple Frog artwork.
2. **Validated SVG derivatives** — production-quality scalable variants.
3. **Raster derivatives** — compatibility/delivery variants.
4. **Historical/legacy variants** — preserved separately and not treated as canonical without explicit validation.

The checkerboard transparency preview must never be treated as artwork.

`EST. 2024`, if present in a supplied historical asset, must not be treated as a canonical factual establishment-date claim until independently verified.

## 6. Brand architecture principle

```text
Brand
  ≠
Domain model
  ≠
Authorization
  ≠
Capability contract
  ≠
Storage provider
  ≠
AI provider
  ≠
Hardware provider
  ≠
Surface
```

A brand change must never force a technical dependency, and a technical provider change must never redefine the brand.

## 7. Conservation integrity

The Purple Frog must not use species status as decorative marketing language.

When a public experience makes a factual species claim, preserve:

- scientific name;
- common name;
- conservation category, if stated;
- geographic/assessment scope;
- assessment date/version, where available;
- authoritative source;
- data/media usage rights.

## 8. Future species-awareness model

The long-term platform should model species as structured resources:

```text
Species
  ↓
Taxonomy
  ↓
Common names
  ↓
Conservation status
  ↓
Habitat / range
  ↓
Threats
  ↓
Evidence & source
  ↓
Conservation action
  ↓
Public awareness
```

This keeps the brand experience evidence-aware and reusable across future product surfaces.

## 9. Governance gate

Before publishing species-related claims or distributing brand artwork:

- verify scientific identity;
- verify the relevant current source and scope;
- verify media licensing/usage rights;
- preserve provenance;
- distinguish symbolic brand language from factual scientific claims;
- maintain trademark and copyright clearance separately from software licensing.
